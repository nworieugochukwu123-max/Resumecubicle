Before and after

Same experience. Clearer story.

Hiring teams skim. Flip the switch to see how a line changes when it leads with the result.

CV bullet points

  • Responsible for managing social media.Swaps a duty for a result, with a number and a timeframe.
  • Helped customers with their problems.Shows volume and quality instead of general helpfulness.
  • Worked in a team on projects.Names the scale and your part in it.
  • Did sales for the company.Shows the outcome and how you achieved it.

LinkedIn headlines

  • Marketing graduate | Looking for opportunitiesAdds the skills recruiters actually search for.
  • Accountant at ABC LtdDescribes what you do, not only where you work.
  • Hard-working professionalReplaces a vague claim with a clear role and skills.

Cover letter openings

  • I am writing to apply for the position advertised on your website.Opens with the match between you and the role.
  • I believe I would be a good fit because I am hardworking and passionate.Proves the quality with a specific example.
  • Please find my CV attached for your consideration.Points the reader to what matters and invites a conversation.

All examples are for illustration only. Your results and wording will be your own, and we never add anything that isn’t true.

Three habits behind every rewrite.

Lead with the result

Start with what changed because of you, then explain how. Readers who stop after a few words should still learn something useful.

Be specific

Numbers, timeframes, team sizes and tools make a claim believable. Where there’s no number, scale and frequency work too.

Write for the reader

Every line should answer what the employer wants to know. If it doesn’t help with the role you’re applying for, it can go.
